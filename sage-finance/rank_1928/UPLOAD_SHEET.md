## Description
$100 invested in 1928 in stocks, bonds, gold, homes or cash, ranked by what it was worth at the end of 2025.

The S&P 500 (dividends reinvested) turned $100 into about $1.16 million, about 10% a year. Baa corporate bonds reached about $54,000, gold about $21,000, 10-year Treasury bonds over $7,700, home prices about $5,600 (price only, no rent) and 3-month Treasury bills about $2,600. Cash kept in a drawer is still $100 but buys only what $5.31 did in 1928, and stocks lost 43.84% in 1931 alone.

Source: Aswath Damodaran, "Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds, Bills, Real Estate and Gold," NYU Stern (January 2026 update, 1928–2025); U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, CPI-U annual averages (1928: 17.1; 2025: 321.943). Figures rounded. Small-cap stocks did even better in the same data, with bigger swings, and aren't shown.
Not financial advice.
