Why "how to write a check" won:
- It's searched a lot: about 190K US searches a month on Google, and it's the #1 Google suggestion for "how to w".
- YouTube autocomplete shows a long tail of extra searches: "to someone", "to yourself", "for rent", "with cents", "for mobile deposit". This video covers rent and cents. Mobile deposit needs an endorsement, which is queued as video 2.
- A check is a flat object, so the animation can show every step exactly. A tie or a tire needs real footage.
- The people searching it are new grown-ups (first apartment, first rent check). That's exactly who an "explained like you're 5" channel is for, and it's money-adjacent, which advertisers pay more for.
- "explained like you're 5" is a real YouTube search phrase (it shows up in YouTube autocomplete).

## Script (as voiced, 62.3 s)

1. First check? It's just six spots.
2. A check is a note to your bank: pay this person.
3. Spot one, top right: today's date.
4. Spot two, pay to the order of: who gets the money.
5. Spot three: the amount in numbers. Squeeze it right up against the dollar sign,
6. so this sneaky raccoon can't sneak an extra number in.
7. Spot four: the same amount, in words. Eight hundred fifty, and zero zero over one hundred.
8. Then draw a line to the end, so nobody can add more.
9. Secret: if the words and the numbers don't match, the bank usually trusts the words. So make them match.
10. Spot five, the memo: what it's for, like your apartment number. It's optional.
11. Spot six, the big one: sign your name. No signature, no money.
12. Always use a pen, and never sign a blank check. That's like handing the raccoon your piggy bank.
13. The numbers at the bottom are your bank's routing number and your account number. Leave them alone.
14. Six spots, and you just wrote a check. Which spot was new to you?
15. Follow for more grown-up stuff.
16. So next time your landlord says, just write me a check... *(loops back to line 1)*

The example is a first rent check: $850.00 to "Oak Street Rentals", memo "Apt 4B rent". The raccoon tries to squeeze a "1" in front of the amount and to add "and 1,000" after the words, and gets blocked both times.

### What changed after the review (second cut)

An independent reviewer scored the first cut 6/10. Fixed in this cut:
- The last "0" of each amount was cut off ("25.5C"). The handwriting no longer clips.
- Faster, active opening: the raccoon peeks over the check on frame 1, and the first spot now starts at 5.1 s (was 6.7 s).
- A rent check instead of a pizza check, since the viewer is paying rent. It also teaches "00/100".
- Close-ups now show the printed labels ("Pay to the order of", "Memo", "Dollars"), and the voice says "pay to the order of" and "routing number".
- The raccoon's tries are shown, not just told.
- "So make them match" added after the words-win rule, because a mismatch can get a check returned.
- The separate follow card is gone. "Follow for more" sits over the recap, and the ending is 1.5 s shorter.
- Not changed: the empty band at the bottom of the frame. YouTube's title, channel name and buttons cover it on phones.

Fact check:
- Words beat numbers: UCC section 3-114, "words prevail over numbers" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/3/3-114). The script says "usually" because a bank can also return a check that doesn't match.
- Routing number = which bank (its "name tag"), then the account number, then the check number. The memo is optional. A check without a signature isn't valid.
- Every name and number on the check is made up: Jamie Lee, Oak Street Rentals, Piggy Bank & Trust, 123456789 (not a valid routing number).
